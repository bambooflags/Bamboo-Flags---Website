Microsoft 365 outage: how to claim your SLA service credit

Microsoft 365 was down for more than 24 hours on 12 and 13 November 2026. Business plans can claim a service credit until 31 December. How to check and file.

Bamboo Flags14 Nov 20263 min read

On 12 November 2026, a global outage stopped Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services. Users reported the first problems just after 12:00 US Eastern time, and the outage spread to more than 70 countries. On 13 November, after more than 24 hours, Microsoft reported broad recovery.

If your business pays for a Microsoft 365 business plan, you may be owed a credit on your Microsoft bill. Microsoft does not apply the credit automatically. You must file a claim by 31 December 2026.

What happened on 12 and 13 November

Microsoft first said that servers failed automated health checks and became unavailable. The Register reported that Exchange Online, Teams meetings, Cloud PCs, Copilot and about 15 other services were affected in North America, Europe and Asia Pacific. Several fixes did not hold, and by the evening of 13 November the outage had passed 27 hours.

On 13 November, a Microsoft spokesperson told WIRED that the cause was "an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade" and not a cyberattack. Microsoft promised a full post-incident report.

To keep delayed mail, Microsoft extended the email timeout to 48 hours.

Who can claim a Microsoft 365 service credit

The Service Level Agreement for Microsoft Online Services (SLA) covers business subscriptions to Office 365, Microsoft 365, Dynamics 365, Intune and Azure.

Personal plans, such as Microsoft 365 Family, are not covered. WIRED explains that the consumer agreement gives refunds only at Microsoft's "sole discretion".

If you bought through an Open licence or with a product key for an Office 365 Small Business Premium suite, the SLA gives the credit as extra days of service, not as money.

How the Exchange Online credit is calculated

For Exchange Online, the SLA counts downtime as the time when users "are unable to send or receive email with Outlook Web Access". Downtime is measured in user-minutes: the length of each incident multiplied by the number of users affected.

The uptime percentage for the month sets the credit:

  • Below 99.9%: 25% of the fees for that service for the month.
  • Below 99%: 50%.
  • Below 95%: 100%.

November has 43,200 minutes. If all your users could not use email for 18 hours, uptime is 97.5%, and the credit is 50%. WIRED uses this example. Microsoft decides whether a credit is owed after an investigation, typically within 45 days. Teams and other services have separate service levels in the same SLA.

The credit applies only to the fees for the service that missed its level. It is the only remedy in the SLA: it does not pay for lost revenue or other losses. The SLA also says that Microsoft's outage messages are "not a confirmation of missed Service Levels".

How to file the claim

The SLA says that the claim goes to Microsoft customer support. It must include:

  • a description of the incident
  • the time and duration of the downtime
  • the names of the affected resources
  • the number and locations of affected users
  • a description of the errors

WIRED filed its own claim in the Microsoft 365 admin center: the headset icon at the bottom right opens Help & support, the headset icon at the top opens Contact support, then choose Email. WIRED suggests that you add the incident codes EX1550543 (Exchange), MO1550821 (Outlook) and TM1550488 (Teams). You get a case number after you submit.

For an incident in November, Microsoft must receive the claim and all required information by 31 December 2026.

What to do this week

  1. 01Confirm that your subscription is a business plan.In the admin center, open Billing, then Bills & payments, and note the November fees for each service.
  2. 02Write down the downtime for your business.Record the hours, the number of users, their locations and the error messages while people still remember them.
  3. 03File the claim before 31 December 2026.If you buy Microsoft 365 through a reseller, ask the reseller how to file. Keep the case number with your November invoice.
  4. 04Check the mail from 12 and 13 November.Look for bounces and for replies that did not arrive, and send important messages again. For another cause of Outlook bounces, see our article on Outlook and DMARC.
  5. 05Write down how staff reach each other and your customers when Microsoft 365 is down.During this outage, some people could not make bank transfers because their verification codes did not arrive.
