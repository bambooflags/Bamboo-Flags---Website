Old Bitly links hijacked: check the short links you published

On 15 November 2026, old Bitly links in Burger King and McDonald's posts sent readers to unrelated pages. How to check the short links your business uses.

Bamboo Flags16 Nov 20263 min read

On 15 November 2026, old Bitly short links in posts from Burger King, McDonald's and Donald Trump started to send readers to unrelated pages. Nobody logged in to the brand accounts. Someone created new Bitly links with the same short codes as links that no longer worked.

If your business put a bit.ly link in a social post, a flyer, a QR code or an email signature, that link can have the same problem. The post stays yours, but the destination can belong to someone else.

What happened to the Burger King and McDonald's links

Ransomnews reconstructed the timeline from Bitly's own link data. A Burger King post from 24 July 2011 promoted a restaurant locator with a bit.ly link. Bitly shows that this link was created at 05:32 UTC on 15 November 2026. It opened the Wikipedia list of Nazi concentration camps.

A McDonald's post from 7 June 2011 had a link that was created 18 minutes earlier. It opened the Wikipedia article on the September 11 attacks. One post that quoted the Burger King tweet had about 2.2 million views. By 19:22 UTC, Bitly showed "Link Deactivated" for both links.

Ransomnews also checked 97 Bitly links in Trump's posts from 2009 to 2013:

  • 40 still opened their original pages.
  • 49 were created again later and opened pages that match the post. 48 of these were created within 17 minutes on 26 May 2023.
  • 7 opened unrelated pages. Four were created on 15 November 2026. Three, from 2024 and 2025, opened domains that imitate crypto services.
  • 1 was blocked by Bitly as spam or malicious.

According to Ransomnews, CNN reported the same problem in old posts from Bill Gates, Justin Trudeau, the FBI and the US Department of Defense. Ransomnews did not check those links itself.

How a dead short link can be claimed again

A bit.ly link has two parts: the domain and the short code after the slash. Bitly calls the code the "back-half". Whoever owns the back-half decides where the link goes. If an old link is deleted and someone else creates the same back-half, every post that contains it now sends readers to the new page.

Bitly's help pages say that back-halves cannot be used again, also after an account is deleted. The creation dates from 2023 to 2026 on links from 2009 and 2011 show that this did not hold. Bitly also deletes accounts that are inactive for three years. Ransomnews names this as one possible route, but the cause is not confirmed.

In February 2025, Disruptionist reported a Bitly bug that let users claim a back-half after the original link or account was deleted. Scammers used it to send old Trump links to a memecoin page. Bitly fixed the bug.

On the evening of 15 November, Bitly said on X that it had found "an issue that allowed bad actors to repurpose certain old, inactive links". It suspended the accounts involved and started to disable the affected links. The statement did not say how many links were affected.

Expired domains have the same risk

Two of the crypto domains in the Trump links were registered after the links were made. One was registered on 15 November 2026. The security company Acunetix calls this broken link hijacking. When a domain expires, "all links that include this domain may be used by an attacker." This also applies to a campaign domain that your business did not renew.

What to do this week

  1. 01List every short link your business published.Search old social posts, flyers, menus, packaging, QR codes, PDFs and email signatures for bit.ly and other shorteners.
  2. 02Open each link in a private browser window and confirm that it opens your page.Repeat the check from time to time, because a link can change after you publish it.
  3. 03Log in to the Bitly account that made the links and keep it active.Bitly deletes accounts after three years without activity. It is not confirmed that an active account protects old links.
  4. 04For new print and QR codes, use an address on your own domain, such as yourbusiness.com/menu, and redirect from there.You control that redirect, and you can change it without a reprint.
  5. 05Keep old campaign domains registered, or remove the links that point to them.
  6. 06If a link opens a page that is not yours, report it to Bitly and edit or delete the post.For a wider security baseline, see our article on the NIST small business cybersecurity guide.
